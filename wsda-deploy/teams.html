The organizational problem

Your analysts have AI access. That is not the same as an AI workflow.

Most organizations have rolled out AI tools and awareness training. What usually is missing is the layer in between: a shared, repeatable way for analysts to use AI inside the work itself, check what it produces, and stand behind the result in front of a stakeholder.

AI output still requires validation

A query or summary that looks right is not the same as one that is right. Analysts need a habit of checking AI output against the data before it reaches a report.

Ad hoc use does not scale

When every analyst improvises their own approach, quality varies by person. Teams need workflows they can repeat, review and teach to new hires.

Technical skill alone is not enough

Knowing SQL or Excel is the starting point. The harder skill is judgment: framing the question, spotting a wrong answer, and knowing when AI is the wrong tool.

Findings have to survive the meeting

AI-assisted work only creates value when an analyst can explain where the number came from and why it can be trusted.

The WSDA PVC methodology

Walter built PVC from two decades of watching analysts succeed and struggle in real workplace conditions. It gives a team one shared way to work with AI.

  1. P
    Prompt with precisionGive AI the business context it needs to produce something useful for the question at hand.
  2. V
    Validate before trustingCheck AI-generated SQL, formulas and analysis against the data itself, not against whether it looks right.
  3. C
    Communicate with confidenceTranslate AI-assisted findings into language stakeholders can act on, and that the analyst can defend.
